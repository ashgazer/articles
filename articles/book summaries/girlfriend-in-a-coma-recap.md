# Girlfriend in a Coma — Recap to Karen's Awakening

*Douglas Coupland · rebuilt up to the moment right after Karen wakes, feeling release and confusion, and realising her words have already annoyed the American press. **Nothing beyond that scene is revealed.***

---

## Where you are

Karen McNeil has just opened her eyes after very nearly two decades unconscious. Emotionally and mentally she's still the seventeen-year-old who went under — which means her own daughter, born while she was comatose, is now catching up to her in age. She's disoriented, flooded with relief at simply *being back*, and already unsettled: whatever she's just said has ruffled the American reporters who've descended on the story. That's exactly your page.

---

## The setup, thread by thread

### The party, the letter, and the coma (1979)
Karen, seventeen, living in North Vancouver, has been having vivid, dread-soaked visions of a bleak future — enough that before a big teenage house-wrecking party, she writes it all down in a letter and hands it to her boyfriend, **Richard Doorland**. Her message, essentially: she'd rather sleep for a thousand years than live to see what she's seen coming. At the party she overdoses on pills and alcohol and drops into a coma nothing can pull her out of. Doctors soon discover she's pregnant with Richard's baby.

### Jared, gone but not gone
Around that same time, their friend **Jared** — a popular, promiscuous high-school jock — dies fast of leukemia, barely giving anyone time to process it. He doesn't really leave the story, though: pieces of the book are narrated by him from wherever he's ended up, a wry, detached ghost watching the rest of the group age without him.

### Megan, growing up in the gap
Karen's daughter, **Megan**, is born while her mother is still comatose and grows up largely in the household of Karen's parents, with Richard present but himself unravelling (see below). She turns out broody and death-fixated, and drifts into a relationship with a boyfriend the family regards as rough company.

### The friends, roughly seventeen years on
None of them end up where they expected to:
- **Richard** — Karen's boyfriend and Megan's father — spends the gap years sliding into serious alcoholism, working on the margins of film/TV, all while carrying the guilt and grief of both Karen's coma and Jared's death.
- **Hamilton** becomes a demolition expert and slides into heroin addiction.
- **Pamela** becomes a glamorous, in-demand supermodel — and a fellow addict alongside Hamilton. The two of them end up together.
- **Wendy**, the one who channelled her intensity somewhere functional, becomes an ER doctor.
- **Linus** disappears from the group for long stretches, drifting the world (a lot of it spent in Las Vegas) chasing some kind of meaning, and comes back feeling emptier than when he left. He and Wendy eventually pair up.

The overall shape of these years: success, glamour, medicine, marriage — all of it achieved, none of it filling the hole. That flatness and dread is the book's engine as much as the coma itself.

### Karen wakes
After all that time, with no clear trigger the book bothers to explain, Karen simply comes back — into a 1997 she doesn't recognise, surrounded by friends now well into their thirties while she is, in every way that matters, still the teenager who went under. The gap between her frozen inner age and the aged-up world around her is immediate and disorienting. Because a teenager waking from a coma after almost twenty years is a sensational story, press attention — including American reporters — has already arrived, and Karen, still reeling, has already said something to them that's landed badly.

## The threshold — where I stop

Karen is awake: relieved, confused, and aware she's just annoyed the Americans with whatever she told them. I'll leave you exactly there.
