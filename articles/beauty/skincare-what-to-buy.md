# Skincare — What to Buy

Prices are rough and UK-based. Aimed at melanin-rich skin, where the main concern is post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation, so everything here errs gentle.

## Cleanser

Just face wash — removes sweat, oil, sunscreen and grime. Not bar soap, which is alkaline and strips the barrier. "Gentle" means your face doesn't feel tight or squeaky afterwards.

- **CeraVe Hydrating Cleanser** (~£10) — standard recommendation, hard to beat
- La Roche-Posay Toleriane — equivalent
- Cetaphil Gentle — equivalent

## Sunscreen

Highest-value item on the whole list. SPF 30+, every day. Modern chemical filters (Korean, Japanese, or newer European formulations) go on clear — mineral sunscreens leave a white cast on brown skin, which is why people give up on them.

## Niacinamide

Easiest as part of a moisturiser rather than a separate step.

- **CeraVe Facial Moisturising Lotion** — already contains niacinamide, so moisturiser and active in one
- The Inkey List Niacinamide (~£8) — standalone
- Geek & Gorgeous B-Bomb — standalone
- The Ordinary 10% + Zinc — cheap and popular, but 10% irritates some people. 4–5% is plenty.

## Azelaic acid

- **The Ordinary 10% Suspension** (~£10) — budget entry. Gritty texture, pills under sunscreen for some people.
- **Paula's Choice 10% Booster** (~£38) — much nicer to use
- **Skinoren 20% / Finacea 15%** — prescription, and the properly effective version. GP can prescribe. If pigmentation is the main target, this is the route with real evidence behind it.

## Retinoid

Start lowest. This is the one that causes irritation.

- **Geek & Gorgeous A-Game 5** (0.05% retinal, ~£15) — excellent value
- **Medik8 Crystal Retinal 1** (~£35) — gentle, well formulated
- La Roche-Posay Retinol B3 — also fine

Don't jump straight to a high-strength retinol.

**Adapalene** is a good option for both pigmentation and breakouts, but prescription-only in the UK. Worth asking about alongside the azelaic acid.

## Order to introduce them

1. **Cleanser + niacinamide moisturiser + sunscreen.** Four weeks. This alone fixes a surprising amount.
2. **Add azelaic acid**, mornings. Four to six weeks.
3. **Add the retinoid**, twice a week at night, building up slowly. Expect some dryness in the first fortnight.

Don't start at step three. Irritation on melanin-rich skin means pigment, which is the thing you're trying to avoid.

## One active at a time

An "active" is any ingredient doing something biological rather than just cleaning or moisturising — retinoids, azelaic acid, vitamin C, exfoliating acids, benzoyl peroxide.

Two reasons to add them one at a time:

- **Diagnostic** — start four at once and your face goes red, and you've no idea which one did it
- **Barrier damage** — stacking actives is how people end up over-exfoliated, which leaves pigment behind

Use one for 4–6 weeks before considering another. Actives work over months anyway, so nothing is lost by going slowly.

## The daily routine (once everything's phased in)

### Morning

1. **Cleanse** — or just rinse with water if your skin's on the dry side. Overnight you've only accumulated your own oil.
2. **Azelaic acid** — thin layer, whole face rather than dabbing on individual marks. Give it a minute to settle.
3. **Moisturiser** (the niacinamide one)
4. **Sunscreen** — last, always. Two fingers' worth for face and neck, which is more than most people use.

### Evening

1. **Cleanse** — this one matters, since you're removing sunscreen. With a heavier SPF, a cleansing balm first isn't overkill.
2. **Retinoid** — on dry skin, pea-sized amount for the whole face. Avoid the immediate eye area and the corners of the mouth.
3. **Moisturiser**

### Layering rule

Thinnest to thickest, actives before moisturiser. Sunscreen is the only fixed position — last in the morning, over everything.

### Practical notes

- **Azelaic acid in the morning, retinoid at night.** Not because they can't be combined, but because splitting them halves the irritation risk. Retinoids also degrade in UV.
- **Buffering** helps with the retinoid — moisturise first, then apply it on top. Blunts irritation while building tolerance. Work up from twice weekly to every other night. Nightly isn't necessary; three times a week gets most of the benefit.
- **Skip the retinoid the night before and after shaving.** Freshly shaved skin plus a retinoid is a reliable route to irritation.
- **If skin gets red or flaky, drop back a step** rather than pushing through. Cleanser, moisturiser, sunscreen for a few days, then resume at lower frequency.

---

*Not dermatological advice. For persistent melasma or dark marks, a GP referral is worth more than any routine — and avoid over-the-counter "fairness" or skin-lightening creams entirely, as they're frequently adulterated with mercury, steroids, or unlabelled hydroquinone.*
