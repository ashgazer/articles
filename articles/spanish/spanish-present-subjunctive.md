# Spanish Present Subjunctive

The subjunctive isn't a tense — it's a **mood**. You use it not to state facts, but for wishes, doubts, emotions, and things that haven't happened yet. The trick is spotting the **trigger**: a phrase that flips the second verb into subjunctive.

The core pattern is almost always two clauses joined by **que**:
> [trigger] + **que** + [subjunctive verb]

> *Quiero **que** vengas.* — I want you to come. (lit. "I want that you come")

---

## Formation — the "opposite vowel" rule

Take the **yo** form of the present tense, drop the **-o**, and add the *opposite* vowel endings:

- **-ar** verbs take **-e** endings
- **-er / -ir** verbs take **-a** endings

| Person | hablar (→ hable) | comer (→ coma) | vivir (→ viva) |
|---|---|---|---|
| yo | habl**e** | com**a** | viv**a** |
| tú | habl**es** | com**as** | viv**as** |
| él/ella/ud. | habl**e** | com**a** | viv**a** |
| nosotros | habl**emos** | com**amos** | viv**amos** |
| vosotros | habl**éis** | com**áis** | viv**áis** |
| ellos/uds. | habl**en** | com**an** | viv**an** |

*Because it's built from the **yo** form, irregular yo-forms carry in: tener → teng**a**, hacer → hag**a**, decir → dig**a**, salir → salg**a**. Learn the yo form and the subjunctive falls out.*

## The common irregulars (not from yo-form)

Six verbs to memorise whole — mnemonic **"DISHES"**:

| Infinitive | Meaning | Subjunctive (yo) | Full set |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D**ar | to give | dé | dé, des, dé, demos, deis, den |
| **I**r | to go | vaya | vaya, vayas, vaya, vayamos, vayáis, vayan |
| **S**er | to be | sea | sea, seas, sea, seamos, seáis, sean |
| **H**aber | to have (aux) | haya | haya, hayas, haya, hayamos, hayáis, hayan |
| **E**star | to be | esté | esté, estés, esté, estemos, estéis, estén |
| **S**aber | to know | sepa | sepa, sepas, sepa, sepamos, sepáis, sepan |

## The triggers — this is the part that matters

You don't "decide" to use the subjunctive; a trigger phrase forces it. Group them by the **WEIRDO** idea: **W**ishes, **E**motions, **I**mpersonal opinions, **R**ecommendations, **D**oubt, **O**jalá/future-time.

| Trigger type | Example phrase | Full example | Maps to English |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wish / want** | quiero que, espero que | Espero que **vengas** | I hope you come |
| **Emotion** | me alegro de que, es una pena que | Me alegro de que **estés** aquí | I'm glad you're here |
| **Impersonal opinion** | es importante que, es mejor que | Es importante que **descanses** | It's important that you rest |
| **Recommendation / request** | te pido que, sugiero que | Te pido que **esperes** | I ask you to wait |
| **Doubt / denial** | dudo que, no creo que | No creo que **sea** verdad | I don't think it's true |
| **Ojalá** | ojalá | Ojalá **llueva** | I hope / if only it rains |
| **Future time** | cuando, en cuanto, hasta que | Cuando **tenga** tiempo | When I have time (not yet true) |
| **Purpose** | para que, a fin de que | Te lo digo para que **sepas** | I'm telling you so you know |

---

## The key contrast: subjunctive vs indicative

Same conjunction can go either way depending on whether it's **fact** (indicative) or **not-yet / hypothetical** (subjunctive):

| Meaning | Mood | Spanish |
|---|---|---|
| "When I have time" (future, not yet) | subjunctive | Cuando **tenga** tiempo |
| "When I have time" (habit, every time) | indicative | Cuando **tengo** tiempo |
| "I think it's true" (belief) | indicative | Creo que **es** verdad |
| "I don't think it's true" (doubt) | subjunctive | No creo que **sea** verdad |

**Rule of thumb:** if the verb after *que* is a fact that's actually happening, indicative. If it's wished-for, doubted, requested, or not-yet-real, subjunctive.

---

*This also powers the "if…then" hypotheticals via the **imperfect** subjunctive (si tuviera… → if I had…), which is the natural next file once the present subjunctive feels solid.*
