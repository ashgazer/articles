# Spanish Past Tenses (the common ones)

Three past tenses do almost all the everyday work in Spanish. The trick to picking the right one is matching it to the English idea in your head:

- **Pretérito (preterite)** — "I did / I ate / it happened." A finished, specific event.
- **Imperfecto (imperfect)** — "I was eating / I used to eat / it was raining." Ongoing, habitual, or background.
- **Pretérito perfecto (present perfect)** — "I have eaten." Recent past still linked to now.

---

## 1. Preterite — regular formation

For **completed, one-off actions**. The English trigger is usually a plain past: *I spoke, I ate, I lived.*

| Person | -ar (hablar) | -er / -ir (comer / vivir) | Maps to English |
|---|---|---|---|
| yo | habl**é** | com**í** / viv**í** | I spoke / I ate |
| tú | habl**aste** | com**iste** / viv**iste** | you spoke / you ate |
| él/ella/ud. | habl**ó** | com**ió** / viv**ió** | he/she spoke / ate |
| nosotros | habl**amos** | com**imos** / viv**imos** | we spoke / we ate |
| vosotros | habl**asteis** | com**isteis** / viv**isteis** | you all spoke / ate |
| ellos/uds. | habl**aron** | com**ieron** / viv**ieron** | they spoke / ate |

**Reach for it when the English is:** "I *[verb]ed*" as a single finished thing — *Yesterday I called him* (Ayer lo llamé), *We arrived at eight* (Llegamos a las ocho).

## Preterite — the common irregulars

Irregular stem + shared endings (**-e, -iste, -o, -imos, -isteis, -ieron** — no accents on *yo*/*él*).

| Infinitive | Meaning | Stem | yo / él | Maps to English |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| tener | to have | tuv- | tuve / tuvo | I had / he had |
| estar | to be | estuv- | estuve / estuvo | I was / he was |
| poder | to be able | pud- | pude / pudo | I could / managed to |
| poner | to put | pus- | puse / puso | I put / he put |
| saber | to know | sup- | supe / supo | I found out / he found out |
| hacer | to do/make | hic- | hice / **hizo** (c→z) | I did/made / he did |
| querer | to want | quis- | quise / quiso | I wanted (tried) to |
| venir | to come | vin- | vine / vino | I came / he came |
| decir | to say | dij- | dije / dijo (→ dij**eron**) | I said / he said |
| traer | to bring | traj- | traje / trajo (→ traj**eron**) | I brought / he brought |

*Note the meaning shifts: in the preterite, **saber** = "found out", **poder** = "managed to", **querer** = "tried to". These are only in the preterite.*

**Truly irregular (memorise whole):**

| Infinitive | Meaning | yo | él | ellos | Maps to English |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ser / ir | to be / to go | fui | fue | fueron | I was / I went |
| dar | to give | di | dio | dieron | I gave / he gave |

*ser and ir share identical preterite forms — context tells them apart.*

## 2. Imperfect — regular formation

For **ongoing, habitual, or background** past. Very regular; only three irregular verbs in the whole language.

| Person | -ar (hablar) | -er / -ir (comer / vivir) | Maps to English |
|---|---|---|---|
| yo | habl**aba** | com**ía** / viv**ía** | I was speaking / I used to eat |
| tú | habl**abas** | com**ías** / viv**ías** | you were speaking / used to eat |
| él/ella/ud. | habl**aba** | com**ía** / viv**ía** | he was speaking / used to eat |
| nosotros | habl**ábamos** | com**íamos** / viv**íamos** | we were speaking / used to eat |
| vosotros | habl**abais** | com**íais** / viv**íais** | you all were speaking / used to eat |
| ellos/uds. | habl**aban** | com**ían** / viv**ían** | they were speaking / used to eat |

**Reach for it when the English is:** "I *used to* [verb]", "I *was* [verb]-ing", or describing how things *were* — *When I was young I lived in Madrid* (Cuando era joven vivía en Madrid), *It was raining* (Llovía).

## Imperfect — the only irregulars

| Infinitive | Meaning | Forms | Maps to English |
|---|---|---|---|
| ser | to be | era, eras, era, éramos, erais, eran | I was / it used to be |
| ir | to go | iba, ibas, iba, íbamos, ibais, iban | I was going / used to go |
| ver | to see | veía, veías, veía, veíamos, veíais, veían | I was seeing / used to see |

That's it — three verbs. Everything else is regular.

## 3. Present perfect — formation

For **recent past that still connects to now**. English trigger: "I *have* [done]."

**haber (conjugated) + past participle.** The participle never changes for gender/number here.

| Person | haber | Maps to English |
|---|---|---|
| yo | he | I have |
| tú | has | you have |
| él/ella/ud. | ha | he/she has |
| nosotros | hemos | we have |
| vosotros | habéis | you all have |
| ellos/uds. | han | they have |

**Regular participles:** -ar → **-ado** (hablado); -er/-ir → **-ido** (comido, vivido).

**Reach for it when the English is:** "I *have* [verb]ed", especially about today/this week/recently — *I have eaten already* (Ya he comido), *Have you seen it?* (¿Lo has visto?). In Spain this is used a lot for anything that feels recent.

## Present perfect — common irregular participles

| Infinitive | Meaning | Participle | Maps to English |
|---|---|---|---|
| hacer | to do/make | hecho | I have done/made |
| decir | to say | dicho | I have said |
| ver | to see | visto | I have seen |
| poner | to put | puesto | I have put |
| volver | to return | vuelto | I have returned |
| escribir | to write | escrito | I have written |
| abrir | to open | abierto | I have opened |
| romper | to break | roto | I have broken |
| morir | to die | muerto | (he/she) has died |
| cubrir | to cover | cubierto | I have covered |
| resolver | to resolve | resuelto | I have resolved |

*Compounds inherit the pattern: descubrir → descubierto, devolver → devuelto.*

## The one that trips English speakers: preterite vs imperfect

Same English sentence can go either way depending on what you mean. Map it like this:

| You want to say... | Tense | Spanish |
|---|---|---|
| "I ate at 8" (one finished event) | preterite | Comí a las ocho |
| "I used to eat at 8" (habit) | imperfect | Comía a las ocho |
| "I was eating when he called" (background + interruption) | imperfect + preterite | Comía cuando llamó |
| "It was night and it was raining" (scenery) | imperfect | Era de noche y llovía |
| "The phone rang" (the event) | preterite | Sonó el teléfono |

**Rule of thumb:** the story rides the **imperfect** for scene-setting ("it was...", "there were...", "I used to...") and drops into the **preterite** each time something actually *happens*.
