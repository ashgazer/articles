- _Regresé mi casa_ → **regresé a mi casa**. Verbs of motion take _a_ before the destination — _fui a, llegué a, volví a_.
- - _En la tarde_ is fine in Latin America; Spain says _por la tarde_. Pick a lane and stay in it.

So: habit, description, states. That's the whole passage.

**The practical test** — try inserting "used to" or "was ___-ing" into the English. _Used to live, used to have, used to go, used to cook, used to like._ If it fits without changing the meaning, it's imperfect. Compare passage 1: "yesterday I used to get up at seven" is nonsense, so preterite.

Where your ear misfired was _every Saturday we went_. English collapses habit and event into one form — "we went" covers both "every Saturday" and "last Tuesday." Spanish splits them. When you see **siempre, todos los, cada, normalmente, a menudo**, that's a habit marker and the verb goes imperfect regardless of how the English looks.


_Íbamos_ is the only one with a written accent — that's true of the imperfect generally (_hablábamos, comíamos_). And note _iba_ covers both _yo_ and _él/ella_, so this is one of the few places where you might actually keep the pronoun to avoid ambiguity.

Worth knowing: **the imperfect has only three irregular verbs in the entire language** — _ser_ (era), _ir_ (iba), _ver_ (veía). Everything else is _-aba_ or _-ía_, no exceptions, no stem changes. It's by far the easiest tense in Spanish, which is a good reason not to dodge it.

Second — _iba_ vs _fui_, which is the choice you were actually making:

- **Íbamos a casa de mi abuela** — we used to go, repeatedly, as a routine.
- **Fuimos a casa de mi abuela** — we went, once, a specific trip.

	Same distinction as everywhere else. _Ir_ isn't special; you just hit it at the moment the habit/event split mattered most.
