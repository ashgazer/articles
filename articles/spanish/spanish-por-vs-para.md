# Spanish Por vs Para

Both often translate to "for," which is why they blur. The clean mental split:

- **PARA** → points *forward* to a goal, destination, purpose, or deadline. Think **→ arrow toward a target.**
- **POR** → points *back* to a cause, or covers movement/exchange/duration along the way. Think **↔ because of / through / in exchange for.**

---

## PARA — destination, purpose, deadline

| Use | Example | English |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose / goal ("in order to") | Estudio **para** aprender | I study (in order) to learn |
| Recipient | Este regalo es **para** ti | This gift is for you |
| Destination | Salgo **para** Madrid | I'm leaving for Madrid |
| Deadline | Lo necesito **para** el lunes | I need it by Monday |
| Opinion ("for me") | **Para** mí, es un error | For me / in my view, it's a mistake |
| Comparison ("for a…") | **Para** ser niño, sabe mucho | For a child, he knows a lot |
| Employment | Trabajo **para** Gousto | I work for Gousto |

**One-line test:** if you can insert "in order to," "destined for," or "by [deadline]" → **para**.

---

## POR — cause, exchange, movement, duration

| Use | Example | English |
|---|---|---|
| Cause / reason ("because of") | Lo hice **por** ti | I did it because of / for you |
| Exchange / price | Lo compré **por** 20 euros | I bought it for 20 euros |
| Duration | Estudié **por** dos horas | I studied for two hours |
| Movement through/along | Caminé **por** el parque | I walked through the park |
| Means / by | Te llamo **por** teléfono | I'll call you by phone |
| "Per" | 60 km **por** hora | 60 km per hour |
| On behalf of / instead of | Firmé **por** mi jefe | I signed on behalf of my boss |
| Passive agent ("by") | Escrito **por** Bulgakov | Written by Bulgakov |

**One-line test:** if you mean "because of," "in exchange for," "through," or "per" → **por**.

---

## The pairs that flip meaning

Same sentence, different preposition, different meaning — these show the logic best:

| Spanish | English | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Lo hago **para** ti | I do it for you (to give you) | you're the goal/recipient |
| Lo hago **por** ti | I do it because of you / for your sake | you're the reason |
| Salgo **para** Madrid | I'm leaving *for* Madrid (destination) | target |
| Paso **por** Madrid | I pass *through* Madrid (en route) | movement through |
| Estudio **para** un examen | I study for an exam (goal) | purpose |
| Estudio **por** la mañana | I study in the morning | duration/time frame |

## Set phrases worth memorising (don't overthink these)

**POR** locks into many fixed expressions:

| Phrase | English |
|---|---|
| por favor | please |
| por fin | finally |
| por eso | that's why |
| por ejemplo | for example |
| por supuesto | of course |
| por lo menos | at least |
| por cierto | by the way |
| por ahora | for now |

**PARA** fixed ones:

| Phrase | English |
|---|---|
| para siempre | forever |
| para nada | not at all |
| ¿para qué? | what for? |
| estar para | to be about to / in the mood for |

---

## The 5-second decision flow

1. Is it a **goal, destination, recipient, or deadline**? → **PARA** (arrow toward target)
2. Is it a **cause, exchange, duration, or route through**? → **POR**
3. Still stuck? Ask "am I pointing *toward* something (para) or explaining *why / through what* (por)?"

*Rule of thumb: **para** looks ahead, **por** looks back or moves through.*
